Part 1

Fixed Prepositions (High-Frequency TPSC Rules)

22 One-Liners
  • ⚡Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, anterior, posterior, prefer) always take 'to', never 'than'.
  • ⚡Transitive verbs in active voice (discuss, describe, comprise, investigate, order, reach, resemble, attack, sign, await) take NO preposition.
  • ⚡'Comprise' = 'consist of' (in active voice, never write 'comprises of'; in passive voice, 'is comprised of' is occasionally used).
  • ⚡'Die of' is used for a disease (die of malaria/cholera), whereas 'die from' is used for an external cause (die from overwork/wounds/starvation).
  • ⚡'Agree with' is used for a person; 'agree to' is used for a proposal/plan; 'agree on' is used for a matter/terms.
  • ⚡'Angry with' / 'annoyed with' is used for a person; 'angry at' / 'annoyed at' is used for a thing, behaviour, or situation.
  • ⚡'Blind in' refers to physical blindness in an eye (blind in the left eye); 'blind to' means ignoring faults or shortcomings.
  • ⚡'Abide by' means to obey or follow a rule, law, or decision.
  • ⚡'Accused of', 'acquitted of', 'convicted of', 'guilty of', and 'innocent of' all take the fixed preposition 'of'.
  • ⚡'Charged with' is used in legal contexts (charged with a crime/offence), whereas 'accused' takes 'of'.
  • ⚡'Prevent from', 'prohibit from', 'abstain from', 'refrain from', and 'debar from' take 'from' + Gerund (V-ing).
  • ⚡'Congratulate on' is always used for achievements (congratulate someone on success, never 'for').
  • ⚡'Insist on' takes 'on' + V-ing (insisted on inspecting the logs).
  • ⚡'Deal in' means to trade in goods/commodities; 'deal with' means to handle a person, problem, or subject.
  • ⚡'Between' is used for two entities (followed by 'and'); 'among' is used for more than two entities.
  • ⚡'Beside' means by the side of (next to); 'besides' means in addition to.
  • ⚡'Since' denotes a specific point in time (since 1972, since Monday); 'for' denotes a period/duration of time (for 10 years).
  • ⚡'Inquire into' / 'look into' means to investigate a matter thoroughly.
  • ⚡'Preside over' means to chair or lead an official meeting.
  • ⚡'Indispensable to', 'essential to', 'tantamount to', 'akin to', and 'conducive to' all take the preposition 'to'.
  • ⚡'Endowed with', 'blessed with', 'compatible with', 'comply with', and 'cope with' take the preposition 'with' (never 'cope up with').
  • ⚡'Consist of' means to be made up of; 'consist in' means to lie or reside in as the main essence.
Part 2

Articles (A, An, The & Zero Article)

11 One-Liners
  • ⚡Indefinite article 'a' vs 'an' is governed strictly by the initial phonetic sound (vowel vs consonant sound), NOT the written letter.
  • ⚡Words starting with a '/juː/' ('yoo') or '/w/' consonant sound take 'a': a university, a union, a European, a uniform, a unique key, a one-rupee note, a one-eyed man.
  • ⚡Words starting with a silent 'h' take 'an': an hour, an heir, an honest officer, an honourable post.
  • ⚡Acronyms starting with a vowel sound (/ɛf/, /ɛm/, /ɛs/, /ɛl/, /eɪtʃ/) take 'an': an FIR, an M.Tech, an SQL query, an HTTP request, an LL.B., an SDO, an MLA.
  • ⚡Use 'the' before mountain ranges (the Himalayas, the Jampui Hills), rivers (the Manu, the Gomati), oceans/seas/gulfs, and island groups (the Andamans).
  • ⚡Do NOT use 'the' before single mountain peaks (Mount Everest, Betalongchhip) or single islands (Majuli, Sri Lanka).
  • ⚡Use 'the' before superlative adjectives (the fastest access speed), ordinal numbers (the first phase), and musical instruments (play the flute).
  • ⚡Do NOT use any article before names of sports/games (play cricket), languages (speak Kokborok/English), or academic subjects.
  • ⚡Use 'the' in parallel comparative constructions: 'The higher the bandwidth, the lower the latency.'
  • ⚡Use 'the + Adjective' to represent an entire class of people as a plural noun: 'The rich', 'The poor', 'The blind' (takes a plural verb).
  • ⚡Use 'the' when a proper noun is used metaphorically as a common archetype: 'Kalidasa is the Shakespeare of India.'
Part 3

Spotting of Errors & Subject-Verb Agreement

14 One-Liners
  • ⚡First-Subject Rule: When subjects are joined by 'as well as', 'along with', 'together with', 'in addition to', 'besides', 'accompanied by', the verb agrees with the FIRST subject.
  • ⚡Proximity Rule: When subjects are joined by 'neither...nor', 'either...or', 'not only...but also', the verb agrees with the NEAREST subject.
  • ⚡Distributive & Indefinite Pronouns (Each, every, either, neither, everyone, somebody, nobody, many a, more than one) always take a SINGULAR verb.
  • ⚡'One of the + Plural Noun' takes a singular main verb: 'One of the servers is down.'
  • ⚡'The number of + Plural Noun' takes a SINGULAR verb ('has'), whereas 'A number of + Plural Noun' takes a PLURAL verb ('have').
  • ⚡Negative Adverb Inversion: Sentences starting with 'Hardly', 'Scarcely', 'Barely', 'No sooner', 'Seldom', 'Never' require Auxiliary + Subject inversion ('Hardly had he...').
  • ⚡Correlative Pairings: 'Hardly/Scarcely' always pairs with 'when' (never 'than'); 'No sooner' always pairs with 'than' (never 'when').
  • ⚡Uncountable Nouns (equipment, information, furniture, advice, luggage, machinery, scenery, evidence, poetry, bread) are always singular and never take '-s' or 'a/an'.
  • ⚡Plural-Only Nouns (cattle, police, gentry, peasantry, clergy, vermin, scissors, trousers) always take a plural verb.
  • ⚡Type-1 Real Conditional: 'If + Simple Present (V1)' -> 'will/can/may + V1'.
  • ⚡Type-2 Unreal Present Conditional: 'If + Simple Past / Subjunctive were' -> 'would/could + V1' (e.g., 'If I were the CISO, I would...').
  • ⚡Type-3 Unfulfilled Past Conditional: 'If + Past Perfect (had + V3)' -> 'would have + V3' (never put 'would have' inside the If-clause!).
  • ⚡'Unless' (= if not) and 'Until' are inherently negative; never use 'not' or 'do not' inside an 'unless/until' clause.
  • ⚡After 'It is high time' or 'It is time' followed by a subject, always use the Simple Past tense (V2): 'It is high time we upgraded the server.'
Part 4

Synonyms, Antonyms, Idioms, Qualifying Words & Voice

18 One-Liners
  • ⚡Ephemeral / Transient = short-lived, fleeting (Antonym: Perpetual, eternal, enduring).
  • ⚡Pragmatic = practical, realistic, sensible (Antonym: Idealistic, utopian, impractical).
  • ⚡Obfuscate = make obscure, unclear, confuse (Antonym: Elucidate, clarify, explicate).
  • ⚡Ubiquitous / Pervasive = omnipresent, found everywhere (Antonym: Rare, scarce, sporadic).
  • ⚡Meticulous / Fastidious / Scrupulous = extremely careful with details (Antonym: Negligent, sloppy, remiss).
  • ⚡Mitigate / Alleviate / Assuage = lessen severity or pain (Antonym: Aggravate, exacerbate, intensify).
  • ⚡Candour = frankness, sincerity, honesty (Antonym: Guile, duplicity, deceit).
  • ⚡Verbose / Garrulous / Loquacious = excessively wordy (Antonym: Laconic, succinct, concise, reticent).
  • ⚡Benevolent = kindly, charitable (Antonym: Malevolent, malicious); Audacious = bold (Antonym: Timid, pusillanimous).
  • ⚡Idiom 'A bolt from the blue' = a sudden, unexpected, and shocking event.
  • ⚡Idiom 'A white elephant' = a costly possession that is burdensome to maintain with little utility.
  • ⚡Idiom 'Bury the hatchet' = end a quarrel and make peace; 'Burn the midnight oil' = work or study late into the night.
  • ⚡Idiom 'At the eleventh hour' = at the very last moment; 'By leaps and bounds' = very rapidly.
  • ⚡Idiom 'Read between the lines' = find hidden meaning; 'Once in a blue moon' = very rarely; 'Play devil's advocate' = argue against an idea to test it.
  • ⚡Qualifying Words: 'Few / a few / the few' modify countable plural nouns; 'Little / a little / the little' modify uncountable nouns ('few/little' = almost none; 'a few/a little' = some positive quantity).
  • ⚡Qualifying Words: 'Hard' = diligently vs 'Hardly' = scarcely/almost not; 'Late' = not on time vs 'Lately' = recently; 'Farther' = physical distance vs 'Further' = additional.
  • ⚡Confusable Pairs: 'Principal' = main/head vs 'Principle' = rule/law; 'Affect' (verb) = influence vs 'Effect' (noun) = result; 'Stationary' = not moving vs 'Stationery' = office paper.
  • ⚡Passive Voice Preposition Exceptions: 'known to' (not by), 'alarmed/surprised/astonished at', 'pleased/satisfied with', 'contained in'.
